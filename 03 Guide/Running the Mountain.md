# Running the Mountain

The Guide is the mountain's voice, not an opponent at the platform. Give the team enough information to make a real choice: visible hazards, a plausible safe route, and the cost of delay. Ask what they do before naming a tile count. Do not demand a check for ordinary travel, competent preparation, or repeating ground whose danger has been solved.

Before a check, say: **“This gains you ____. A slip costs ____. A true fall returns you to ____.”** Name the last secured anchor. If failure would only make the team try the same throw again, change the scene or skip the check.

Use one or two tiles for most actions. Three tiles means a hard choice; four should be rare and telegraphed. Weather is not simply a difficulty tax. Show what it does to the route: ice seals a crack, rain loosens a traverse, or wind exposes a safer line behind a dangerous one. Let preparation remove a named danger and reduce the count.

On an ordinary failed check, the rope **catches a slip**. Mark the leader's Exposure and the team's Rope Strain, then show the setback without moving everyone to camp. A revealed handhold, a torn map, or a shelter seen from below can all survive failure. If the rope was already at 3 Strain when the check failed, the whole team truly falls. Ask each player for one instant of the cascade, move them to the last anchor, and show what changed. Even that loss should create a new decision rather than erase every discovery.

## First leg: The Bell Traverse

The team leaves the Green Foot for a camp under a ruined weather bell. On one side of the traverse is wet stone above a long drop; on the other, an older stair disappears under a fresh slide. Both reach the bell, but neither is wholly safe.

- **Wet traverse:** two tiles. Strength to brace an anchor or waiting for the rain to pass could reduce it to one. A slip tears loose a handhold and raises Rope Strain; a true fall returns the team to the last pinned rope.
- **Buried stair:** one tile to uncover and test. Success reveals a slower route to the bell; failure exposes a hollow under the slide and blocks the stair until the team braces it.
- **At the bell:** a dry niche can become the next camp. Inside is a slate bearing the names of three climbers who have not yet arrived. Decide whether it is a warning, an error, or evidence of something above.

The team should be able to reach the bell by planning, changing routes, or accepting a fall. Do not make a particular check the only door forward.

## Boundaries and access

Ask the table about injury, exposure, abandonment, and death; anyone can pause a scene. Throwing skill is **not** character competence. A player may direct someone else's throw while choosing the pieces and approach. The same tile count and consequences apply.
