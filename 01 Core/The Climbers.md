# The Climbers

Make one climber each on a 3 × 5-inch [[Climber Card|flash card]]. Write a name, weeks on Aster (start at 0), and a place below you still call home. Then answer in a few words:

- What does eternal life let you keep, or finally let you do?
- Who on this rope would you catch without thinking? Why?
- What did you bring that cannot be replaced?

Circle one **trained skill**. Every climber can attempt all three; the chosen skill gives one tile reduction per leg when it fits the check. Mark it used, then refresh it at the next camp.

- **Strength:** Grip, haul, carry, brace, or set an anchor. Use it when force and control hold the mountain in place.
- **Courage:** Step onto an exposed crossing, enter an unknown gap, or act while afraid. Use it when committing is the obstacle.
- **Willpower:** Endure cold, hunger, sleeplessness, or a long hold. Use it when the challenge is continuing rather than beginning.

Before pieces are flipped, say how the trained skill helps. If it addresses the declared danger, reduce the check by one tile, minimum one. Only the leader's trained skill applies, even when another player makes the throw. A skill does not replace a route, a tool, or an explanation.

Each climber begins with **0 Exposure** and one personal object. Exposure measures injury, cold, and exhaustion. Describe wounds freely; the number is what matters in the rules.

On the back of a card, draw the team as names connected by a single line. Write one promise between each pair of neighbors. The rope is physical gear, but the drawing records who is responsible for whom. The team cannot cut a climber free to cancel a failed check.

## The body learns

Whenever a week passes, increase WEEKS by 1 and add one permanent visible change: callused palms, a wind-bent stance, frost-pale eyelashes, a voice made thin by altitude, or something stranger. It is a record, not a bonus. Add one sentence about what the climber now finds ordinary that would once have frightened them.

You may change your reason for climbing. Do not erase the original answer; cross it out so the team can see what the mountain took.
