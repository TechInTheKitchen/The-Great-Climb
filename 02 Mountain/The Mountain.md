# The Mountain

Mount Aster rises beyond every known map. From its foot, the upper face looks close enough to reach in a few days. From the first high camp, climbers learn that distances on Aster are measured in weeks.

The old promise is simple: **reach the summit and live forever**. No witness agrees about the source. Some speak of a spring beneath the final snow; others of a door that opens only when a rope team arrives together. Guides, pilgrims, physicians, and rulers have spent generations testing the claim. Nobody has brought down proof. Nobody has brought down a convincing denial.

The mountain is not empty. Camps change hands; families are born without seeing the lowlands. Trade runs vertically: lamp oil upward, messages downward. Shrines of bent tools, dead weather stations, and ancient ropes mark the way.

## Five faces of the ascent

- **The Green Foot:** rain, loose soil, rooted stone, and the last reliable roads. Here people still speak of returning.
- **The Broken Stair:** sheer bands, hanging bridges, and camps stacked in old quarry cuts. The first hard week separates visitors from residents.
- **The White Reach:** snowfields over crevasses, blank horizons, and wind that moves a camp while its occupants sleep.
- **The Breathless Wall:** bare rock above most shelter. Voices carry badly; gestures become a language of their own.
- **The Crown:** a dark shape glimpsed only between storms. Its form should remain uncertain until the team earns the sight of it.

These faces are a reading path, not five required levels. A route can cross back through lower terrain; a fall can make yesterday's hard-won altitude unreachable. Let the team draw and annotate its own mountain map.

## What weeks do

Long exposure is ordinary here. Hands harden around rope fibers. Skin cracks, heals, and cracks in the same places. Climbers learn the smell of incoming snow and the exact weight of another person falling. Aster's eeriness should rise from that ordinary adaptation: a veteran may forget the feel of warm rain while remembering every hold on a wall crossed three weeks ago.

Keep the summit's truth open for now. What matters at the start is why these climbers believe it, and what they are willing to keep carrying upward.
